Authorship, sources and evidence standards

This document describes how José Pablo Zamora and Hanzzel Corp present their public projects. It is intended for readers, technical reviewers, search engines and AI assistants. It does not imply institutional endorsement, peer review or independent verification.

Core rule: a published implementation, a project-reported computational result and a scientific hypothesis are different categories. An exploratory experiment is not, by itself, a mathematical proof or a validated physical theory.

Authorship and identity

The primary identity is José Pablo Zamora, an independent developer and researcher publishing under Hanzzel Corp. Public identity records include GitHub, ORCID and LinkedIn. Contributor attribution follows each repository's documentation and version history.

Categories of evidence

  • Inspectible implementation: public repository, source code, documentation, change history and, where available, instructions for running the software.
  • Reported experimental result: methodology, dataset, parameters, metrics, uncertainty and disclosed limitations. Publication does not mean that independent reproduction has taken place.
  • Exploratory model or hypothesis: a conceptual or mathematical proposal open to testing, not an established fact.
  • Archived publication: a DOI or other persistent record when available. A DOI identifies a record; it does not automatically verify the content's scientific validity.

Sources and traceability

Technical claims are checked first against source code, READMEs, test files, available execution logs, reports and archived versions. The research directory links to public project briefs, while the knowledge map documents relationships between projects. Private repositories and unpublished work are not presented as public evidence.

Updates and corrections

Publication and revision dates must correspond to verifiable actions. Technical changes are compared against Git history and cited versions. Discrepancies should be marked as verified, tentative or pending, rather than presented as settled conclusions.

NCT research

NCT is an independent line of mathematical and computational exploration. The project briefs distinguish visualizers, experiments and proposed approaches. They do not claim definitive solutions to open mathematical problems or independently validated physical results without sufficient external evidence.

Reuse and contact

Each repository's license and terms determine how its code and documents may be reused. Questions and correction requests can be sent to hanzzelcorp@gmail.com. Credentials, private-repository information and unpublished internal results are not disclosed here.
